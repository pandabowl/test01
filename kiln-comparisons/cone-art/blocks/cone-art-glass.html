Cone Art Glass Kiln Comparison

Tucker's Cone Art glass kilns are top-loading kilns for fusing, slumping and annealing, with heating elements in the lid. They come in round, square and oval shapes, from the 120-volt 117G and 115G SQ to the 41" × 31" ovals, and most sizes come 9" or 13.5" deep. Every model is CSA/cUL certified. Amps are for 240-volt and 208-volt single-phase power.

How to read a Cone Art glass kiln model number
  • G = glass kiln. SQ = square.
  • Most model numbers give the interior size: the first two digits are the width and the last two are the depth. 09 = 9" deep and 13 = 13.5" deep. The 42 models are ovals, 41" × 31" inside.
  • Cone Art adds the controller in front: B3K = Bartlett 3-button controller, V6 = Bartlett V6-CF 12-key controller.

For example, the 2313G is 23.5" across and 13.5" deep inside.

Which Cone Art glass kiln should I buy?

  • No 240-volt circuitThe 117G plugs into a standard household outlet. The square 115G SQ needs a 20-amp outlet.
  • First full-size glass kilnThe 2309G or the 13.5"-deep 2313G. Both run on a 40-amp breaker at 240V.
  • Square platters and panelsThe 2309G SQ or 2313G SQ, with a 23" × 23" interior.
  • Big work and productionThe 28"-wide 2809G and 2813G, or the 41" × 31" ovals: the 4209G and 4213G.

Controllers and lid lifters

Our Cone Art glass kilns come with Bartlett's 3-button controller. For more control, upgrade to the 12-key Bartlett V6-CF; ask us about the Genesis touchscreen. A lid lifter is an option on the 23" and 28" models and now comes standard on the 42" ovals.
