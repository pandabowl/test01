Evenheat Ceramic Kiln Model Comparison

Evenheat pottery kilns are top-loading electric kilns with a digital controller. They come in two families: RM kilns fire to cone 8 (2300°F), and HF (High Fire) kilns fire to cone 10 (2350°F). The charts below compare every Evenheat ceramic kiln we sell, using Evenheat's published specs for its standard build. That's 240-volt, single-phase power unless a kiln is marked 120V.

Which Evenheat ceramic kiln should I buy?

  • First kiln for a home studioThe RM 1822 for cone 6 work. It's Evenheat's most popular personal-size kiln and plugs into a NEMA 6-50 outlet. Firing to cone 10? Look at the HF 1818, which draws 28 amps on a NEMA 6-30 plug.
  • Studios and schoolsThe RM 2329 (7.5 cu ft, cone 8) is Evenheat's most popular studio kiln. The HF 2327 (7.0 cu ft, cone 10) is its best-selling high fire kiln, with multi-zone control and the Dyna-Lift lid lift.
  • Easy loadingThe 18"- and 22"-deep kilns keep the floor within reach: the HF 2318 and HF 2918 for cone 10, or the RM 2322 and RM 2922 for cone 8, with the rim only 33" up.
  • China painting and porcelainThe RM 1413 is Evenheat's most popular china painting kiln. On a 120-volt outlet, choose the RM 1210 for china paint to cone 07 or the HF 810 for cone 8.
How to read an Evenheat model number
  • RM kilns fire to cone 8 (2300°F). We list them as RM II. HF (High Fire) kilns are built with 3" firebrick and fire to cone 10 (2350°F).
  • On the round kilns from the 1413 up, the first two digits are about the chamber's width in inches and the last two are about its depth.
  • 1210-B models are the 240-volt versions of the small 1210 chamber, built for porcelain bisque as well as china paint. The plain RM 1210 runs on 120 volts.

For example, the HF 2327 is a high-fire kiln about 23" across (23.5") and 27" deep.

RM vs. HF, and controller options

HF (High Fire) kilns use 3" firebrick and fire to cone 10. Evenheat keeps the chamber the same size as the matching RM kiln and makes the outside larger, so standard kiln furniture still fits. The HF 1818 and the larger HF kilns add multi-zone control, with more than one thermocouple for even heat from top to bottom. The HF 2318, 2327, 2918 and 2927 come with Evenheat's Dyna-Lift lid lift assist.

Controllers: Evenheat offers the Icon, Icon+ and TAP II touchscreens and the push-button Rampmaster II; the multi-zone HF kilns use the TAP I, Icon+ or Rampmaster II. Evenheat's Quiet Drive solid-state relay option gives tighter temperature control and longer element life.

Low fire only: the oval RM 2522 and RM 2541 top out at cone 03 (2000°F), so they're for low-fire clay and glazes, not cone 6.
