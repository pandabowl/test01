Brick or fiber, updraft or downdraft

Brick kilns have a firebrick lining with 1½" fiber board backup insulation. Fiber kilns use ceramic fiber insulation with an ITC coating. Laguna says fiber saves up to 30% on yearly fuel costs, heats and cools faster, and has fewer wall-cracking problems than brick.

Updraft or downdraft: In an updraft kiln, the heat leaves through a flue in the top, with a slide damper above it. In a downdraft kiln, the heat travels down through the load and leaves through a flue in the floor.

Every model includes natural-draft, ceramic-tip venturi burners, direct-spark electronic ignition, a digital high-limit safety shut-off, manual burner valves and a water column gauge. A programmable controller (8 programs, 16 segments each) is optional. All Laguna gas kilns are ETL listed by Intertek.

Planning a gas kiln installation
  • Gas pressure: natural gas needs at least 6" water column at the kiln. Propane uses a two-stage regulator (about 5 PSI, then about 15" water column), and your propane supplier sizes the tank.
  • Gas line: a licensed plumber sizes it from the kiln's BTU rating, its distance from the meter and the number of elbows.
  • Clearance: at least 4 ft from combustible walls and 3 ft from noncombustible walls.
  • Venting: indoors, the kiln must be vented, usually with a hood (stainless steel is best). Outdoors, Laguna recommends a noncombustible roof 6 to 8 ft above the flue.
  • Permits and delivery: check with your local building department first. Kilns ship crated, and you'll need a forklift or pallet jack to unload one.
