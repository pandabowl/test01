Paragon Glass Kiln Model Comparison

Paragon makes three kinds of glass-fusing kilns: top-loading Fusion kilns, CS clamshell kilns that lift away so you can arrange glass right on the shelf, and Janus kilns that fire both glass and pottery. For metal clay, enameling and jewelry, it makes small SC, Xpress and Caldera kilns. The charts below compare the most popular Paragon glass and metal clay kilns side by side, using Paragon's spec sheets.

How to read Paragon glass kiln names
  • CS = Clamshell. On S (single-opening) models, the walls and lid lift together. On D (dual-opening) models, you can also lift just the lid and load from the top.
  • The CS number is the interior width: the CS-14D is 14" square, and the CS19S and CS19D are 19" square.
  • Janus = a CT pottery kiln with heating elements in the lid and a switch for glass or pottery firing.
  • Xpress E = front-loading enameling and china painting kilns. SC = small ceramic fiber kilns for silver clay and jewelry.

Which Paragon glass kiln should I buy?

  • First glass kiln on a household outletThe Fusion 14 or the clamshell CS-14D. Both have a 14"-square chamber and plug into a standard 120V outlet. The CS-14D fires up to nine 4" × 4" tiles at once.
  • Larger panels, bowls and plattersThe CS19S or CS19D (19" square), or the Fusion 10 (23.5" round with lid and wall elements) for deeper slumps.
  • Glass and pottery in one kilnA Janus kiln. Glass mode fuses and slumps large pieces on one shelf, and pottery mode fires to cone 10.
  • Metal clay, jewelry and testsThe SC-2, SC-3 or Caldera on 120 volts. For quick tests, our best-selling QuikFire 6 can fire and cool small glass pieces in 30 to 40 minutes.

Windows, bead doors and annealing

The Fusion 10 and Fusion 14 can be ordered with a viewing window. The SC kilns can add a window or a bead door with a mandrel holder, and the Caldera takes a bead collar with a bead door for annealing beads on mandrels. For lampworking, see the F-120 annealer.
