Paragon Pottery Kiln Model Comparison

Paragon's top-loading pottery kilns are the CT (Ceramic Top) series: 8-, 10- and 12-sided kilns with 3" firebrick, plus heavy-duty Viking versions of three of the larger sizes. The Iguana and Dragon load from the front. Every model below fires to cone 10 (2350°F) and comes with Paragon's Sentinel SmartTouch digital controller. The chart compares the most popular Paragon pottery kilns side by side, using Paragon's spec sheets for 240-volt, single-phase power, the most common hookup in homes and schools.

How to read a Paragon CT model number
  • CT = Ceramic Top, Paragon's top-loading pottery series.
  • The digits are the number of sides, then the approximate interior width and depth in inches. Paragon also writes them with slashes, as in "Ceramic Top Loader 12/28/22".
  • Viking = Paragon's Heavy Duty version of a CT kiln: the same chamber with 14,400 watts of power, 60 amps and a direct-wire hookup.
  • The CT models replaced Paragon's TnF "-3" kilns, so our product titles list both names.

For example, the CT122822 is a 12-sided kiln, about 28" wide and 22" deep. It used to be the TnF-27-3.

Which Paragon kiln should I buy?

  • First kiln for a home studioThe CT81722. It holds 2.95 cu ft, fires to cone 10, draws 30 amps and plugs into a NEMA 6-50 outlet. For porcelain, china paint or dolls on a 30-amp circuit, see the CT81713.
  • Most popular all-around kilnThe CT122822. Its 12-sided chamber is 28" wide but only 22.25" deep, so it holds 8.11 cu ft and you can still reach the floor. It's our best-selling Paragon pottery kiln.
  • Heavy daily firingThe Viking 24, 27 or 28. Paragon builds them with 14,400 watts of power and industrial-grade relays and wiring for back-to-back cone 10 firings.
  • Front loadingThe Iguana (4.2 cu ft, plugs into a NEMA 6-50 outlet) or the Dragon 24 (9 cu ft, direct wire). You load through a door instead of reaching down into the kiln.

CT, Viking and Janus: what's the difference?

Every CT kiln has 3" firebrick, solid-state relays and element grooves that need no pins. It also has an auxiliary outlet (AOP) to power an optional VentMaster kiln vent, and it comes pre-drilled for the vent. The 10- and 12-sided models add Paragon's spring-assisted LiteLid, and the CT102329, CT122822 and CT122829 come apart in sections for moving and repairs.

Viking models are the same kilns with more power and heavier electrical parts for frequent cone 10 firing. They draw 60 amps and are wired directly to a circuit, with no plug.

Janus versions of the CT81713, CT102322 and CT122822 fire both glass and pottery. A switch picks glass mode (lid and middle wall elements) or pottery mode (wall elements only, up to cone 10). See our Paragon Janus kilns.
