Skutt Kiln Model Comparison

Skutt KilnMaster pottery kilns come in 8-, 10- and 12-sided sizes, plus an oval production kiln. They're all top-loading and automatic, with a programmable digital controller. The chart below compares the most popular Skutt kiln models side by side. Specs are for 240-volt, single-phase power, the most common hookup in homes and schools.

How to read a Skutt model number
  • KM = KilnMaster digital controller. KMT = KilnMaster Touchscreen controller with built-in Wi-Fi.
  • The first digits are the number of sides (8, 10 or 12). The last two digits are the interior depth in inches.
  • -3 = 3" firebrick instead of the standard 2.5". PK = Production Kiln, with higher-wattage elements and a direct-wire hookup.

For example, the KM1027-3 is a 10-sided kiln, 27" deep, with 3" brick.

2.5" vs. 3" brick, and KM vs. KMT

3" brick (-3 models) holds heat better than standard 2.5" brick, so the kiln reaches temperature more easily and cools more slowly. The trade-off is about 1" less interior width.

KMT models swap the keypad for Skutt's touchscreen controller, which has built-in Wi-Fi, firing graphs and KilnLink monitoring from your phone. Many models are also sold as ready-to-fire packages with a furniture kit and EnviroVent.

Need something smaller? For test tiles, jewelry and small pieces, see the GlazeTech cone 10 test kiln (1.1 cu ft), the KM714 (1.4 cu ft), and the 115-volt KM614-3 (0.8 cu ft).
