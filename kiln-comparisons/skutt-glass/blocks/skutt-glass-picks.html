Which Skutt glass kiln should I buy?

  • First kiln on a household outletThe FireBox 8x6 LT. It fires glass, metal clay and ceramics to cone 6, and comes with a shelf, posts and kiln wash. For bigger flat pieces, the FireBox 14 holds a full square foot of tile or a 12" square plate.
  • Home studio fusing and slumpingThe GM814 or GM1014. Elements in the lid and sides give even heat from top to bottom, which helps with deep slump and drop molds. They need a 50-amp NEMA 6-50 outlet.
  • Classrooms and big panelsThe GM1414. Skutt says it's likely the most widely used kiln among professional glass instructors. Its oval chamber holds a 22" × 39" layer of shelves. For big round platters, see the GM1214-3.
  • Production and multi-layer firingThe GM1018 or GM1227-3. Side elements and zone control let you fire up to 3 or 4 layers of similar work at once.

Lid-and-side vs. side-fired kilns, and GM vs. GMT

Lid and side elements (GM814, GM1014, GM1214-3 and GM1414): these kilns heat from the lid and the walls. The side elements keep the shelf warm and the heat even for deep molds, but Skutt designs these kilns for one shelf level per firing.

Side-fired production kilns (GM818, GM1018 and GM1227-3) heat from the walls only and come apart in sections. Each section has its own thermocouple (Skutt calls this zone control), so the temperature stays even from the top shelf to the bottom. That makes them the right choice for fusing on several shelves, large drop molds and annealing tall pieces.

GMT models are the same kilns with Skutt's GlassMaster Touchscreen controller. It adds Wi-Fi, firing graphs and a program library (more about it below). The GM814 and GM818 are also sold as ready-to-fire packages with a furniture kit.
